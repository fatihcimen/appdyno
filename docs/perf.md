# Performance measurement

Every measurement is read from the platform's own tools and stored with the raw output it came from
(`<measurements dir>/pm-<id>.json` and `.txt`; the MCP server's `--measurements`, default
`~/Library/Caches/appdyno/measurements`). Commands run in the C locale. Nothing leaves the machine.

## What was tried, cell by cell

Xcode 27.1, iOS 27.0.1 on a cable, iOS 26.5 on the simulator, 2026-11-03. Host load beside what depends on it.

**Why launch was slow, simulator.** App Launch with `--launch` records (287 s at host load 30) and its `life-cycle-period` has 0 rows, so attaching was not what emptied it. What reads it is the probe, phase by phase, and a Time Profiler attached to a process that `simctl launch --wait-for-debugger` holds before its first instruction, let go with `SIGCONT` when the recording has started (16 s at load 14).

**Why launch was slow, iPhone.** App Launch with `--launch`. Settings answers "Permission to debug com.apple.Preferences was denied ... signed with 'get-task-allow'" within 4 s, and the measure asks first, so a refusal costs no launches.

**Frames, frozen frames, stalls, iPhone.** A 1 s stall of the main thread in a scroll shows as a hitch of 50 ms and as a hang of 1.02 s in one Animation Hitches recording: no frame was on its way, so nothing was late. A frozen frame there is a hitch or a hang of 700 ms or longer, the two of one moment counted once.

**Leaks, iPhone.** The Leaks template attached. Its table is under `tracks/track/details/detail`, not `data/table`, and has one row per leaked object. Its first look at the heap comes 10 s in: a recording of 5 s reads 0 of 3 retain cycles made, one of 12 s reads all, so it records 15 s. Settings answers "Unable to acquire required task port". An earlier attempt never started at host load 400 and over; at load 7-24 it takes 18 s.

## The gate

Every measure's numbers are kept as records (`packages/perf`): kind, median, samples and spread, source command, raw output
file, device and OS, virtual or physical, build type, compile state, host and device load, thermal state, tool version and
time. A run goes to `<measurements dir>/runs/<app>/<scenario>/<device class>/`. A baseline goes to
`.appdyno/perf/baselines/<app>/<scenario>/<device class>.json` (`APPDYNO_PERF_DIR` moves it), which can be committed. Limits
go in `.appdyno/perf/budgets.json`: a list of `{ "kind": "launch.ttid_ms", "max": 2000 }`, with `app`, `scenario`, `deviceClass`
and `min` optional.

```text
appdyno perf-launch --app A --mode cold --repeats 6      a measured run, kept
appdyno perf baseline --app A                            adds the newest run to the baseline; at least 3 runs are needed
appdyno perf compare --app A [--format text|json|junit]  exit 1 on a regression or a broken budget
appdyno perf budget --app A                              budgets only
```

The same comes through `perf_compare`. A journey keeps its numbers with `keepRun` from `packages/ops`: a scenario and
metrics, and the comparison reads them like any other.

**How a change is called.** All of: at least 5 samples each side and 3 baseline runs, else `few-samples`, which is no alarm; a rank
test on all baseline samples against the run, p under 0.01 (by permutation, because whole milliseconds tie; 4 against 4 cannot reach
it); a shift past a floor by unit (15 % and 10 ms for times); and past 4 standard deviations of the baseline's run medians. A run under
high load or on a hot device says `noisy` and exit 0, and is refused as a baseline. Unlike builds or compile states say `refused`.

**Why that method.** Samples of one run share its load and heat, so two runs of an unchanged app differ "significantly". Across 72
ordered pairs of unchanged runs, a rank test alone and a 10 % difference of medians alone would have alarmed:

| device, clean runs                           | pairs | rank test alone | 10 % of medians alone | the gate's calls, 6 runs |
| -------------------------------------------- | ----- | --------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Android emulator, 6 runs at load 9-16        | 30    | 10              | 24                    | 0                        |
| iOS simulator, 5 runs at load 9-13           | 20    | 2               | 0                     | 0                        |
| iPhone, 9 runs at any host load              | 72    | 10              | 9                     | 0                        |
| iOS simulator, an earlier 9 runs, load 10-15 | 72    | 28              | 25                    | 0                        |

Run medians of the unchanged app: emulator 371-667 ms, simulator 1,661-1,705 ms, iPhone 554-626 ms. On an emulator the same launch read
350 ms and 700 ms minutes apart between load 11 and 16 on 15 cores, and one baseline run at load 41 read 1,661 ms; so a run with host load over
0.8 per core is marked noisy and is refused as a baseline.

Why 4 standard deviations of run medians rather than their range, from `scripts/perf-floor-sim.mjs` (a run moving 6 % by itself, 5 % noise per
launch, 6 launches, 400 trials): false alarms 3.0 % with 3 baseline runs and 2.3 % with 5 for the range, 0.8 % and 0.5 % for 4 sd; +40 % caught
100 % and 99 % against 89 % and 91 %. At 10 % movement no floor is safe with 3 runs (4 sd still 5.8 %), so use more runs on a loaded host, or
a quieter one. These are simulated rates, and what the device runs below show is no false alarm in 6 runs each, not a rate.

**The acceptance tests** (`test-device/perf-compare.test.ts`; the numbers are kept in `.ci/perf-gate.txt`), 6 launches per run, a baseline
of 3 runs, 6 unchanged runs, then the fixture's start made slower by a known amount:

| device            | baseline median | unchanged runs               | false alarms | injected at start | called                      |
| ----------------- | --------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------ | ----------------- | --------------------------- |
| Android emulator  | 491 ms          | 366-764 ms, floor 509 ms     | 0 of 6       | 800 ms            | regression, +553 ms, exit 1 |
| iOS simulator     | 1,673 ms        | 1,659-1,787 ms, floor 251 ms | 0 of 6       | 1,000 ms          | regression, +983 ms, exit 1 |
| iPhone 13 Pro Max | 563 ms          | 553-620 ms, floor 84 ms      | 0 of 6       | 400 ms            | regression, +471 ms, exit 1 |

On 2026-11-03 the iPhone's gate passed once and, at host load 10-38 with other work on the Mac, called one unchanged run in another: 623 ms against 540.5, a shift of 83.5 over a floor of 81. A third run had 5 of 5 unchanged runs ok, then reached its hour's limit at host load over 100. So 0 of 6 is what those runs show, not a rate. The floor was left as it is.

On a Xiaomi the gate gave no verdict: its device load read 14-16 each time, over the limit of 8 (`DEVICE_LOAD` in `packages/perf/src/record.ts`), and its run answered `new`, with no baseline. At rest it read 14.31 with one task running and `procs_blocked` 0: the load was 14 kernel threads in uninterruptible sleep (`wdtk-0` to `wdtk-7`, `hang_detect`, `ccci_poll` and others), which Linux counts though they use no CPU, and `/proc/pressure/cpu` is closed to the shell there. A record now keeps that count, and the limit is applied to the load without it. Run again on 2026-11-04, at host load 250-370: a baseline of 900 ms, 6 unchanged runs of 837-928 ms against a floor of 135 ms and no false alarm, and an injected 800 ms called a regression, +723.5 ms, exit 1. An Android phone's launches do not wait for the Mac in this test, as what `am start -W` reports is the phone's. An iPhone's do: its time runs until the runner reads the first screen, and read 550 ms on a calm Mac and 1.3-3 s at host load 100-600. On 2026-11-04 its shortened gate at load 57-125 called one unchanged run in six a regression, 83 ms past a floor of 1,015 ms, and at load 50-370 missed an injected 400 ms under a floor of 370 ms: at that load it says nothing either way.

An injected change has to clear what that device's runs do by themselves, so a smaller one on a loaded simulator is not called: it is
reported as `ok`, with the floor beside it. On an emulator a sleep at the start of a very fast launch shows only partly: 400 ms added
about 230 ms, because the system's own start work runs alongside it.

Kept traces and runs stay on this machine until removed; nothing prunes them.

## The dashboard

```text
appdyno perf survey --app A [--device S]               every measure on that device in turn, then the page
appdyno perf dashboard [--app A] [--out dir] [--open]  the page alone, from what is kept; no device needed
```

`perf_compare` with `action: dashboard` writes the page too, in its usual place: `<measurements dir>/dashboard/index.html`. `--out` and `--open` are the command line's alone. The page is rewritten after each kept run and each baseline, and an open page reloads itself every minute, which closes a table left open. A failure to write it never fails a measurement.

`perf survey` calls the same operations anyone can, and names no device: what reads each measure is tried in turn until the measure has a number, a refusal is kept as that measure's answer for that app on that kind of device, and the next measure still runs. Every operation keeps its refusals the same way, except what the caller turned off (`probe: false`) and a way the device has another of (no `hangs` where the frame trace reads stalls); a refusal about a part of a row (threads, a CPU profile) stands for no row. A call that ran and kept no number for that measure, or failed for another reason such as a timeout, is printed and not kept: neither says what the device cannot do. One line per measure.

There is no server, and the page is one file with no script, which loads nothing: its content policy allows only its own style. Everything from the store is escaped. It follows the system's light or dark, and prints as ink on paper with no tooltips or tables. An app has a section of its own, with an anchor. In order:

1. **What needs attention**, across every app, for the newest run of each scenario: regressions and broken budgets as `perf compare` calls them, and runs under high load or heat. A debuggable build is said beside its number but is no alarm. It says how many scenarios have a baseline and how many budget rules there are, since "nothing needs attention" means little without them, and which files could not be read.
2. **Measures by device.** Above the table: that an emulator's or a simulator's numbers are relative to this Mac and that real frame rates, frozen frames, hitches and energy need a phone, and, with no phone measured for the app, the command for one. A cell holds the newest reading of that measure on that kind of device, with its unit, kind, scenario and age; an estimate from a model is marked; so is a reading under noise; and on a simulator it says when the number is something else: main-thread pacing for frames, frozen frames and stalls, this Mac's energy. A refusal newer than any reading gives its reason instead. Otherwise "not measured yet". A kind no measure answers has a row of its own under "other". Emulators and simulators read as relative, and the page says so.
3. **Trends**, for a kind with more than one run on a device: one small chart per kind, scenario and device, each with one y-scale, from zero, with the runs evenly spaced. The shaded band is the baseline's median plus and minus the floor of the gate above: a run outside it has moved past the floor, though the call needs the rank test as well. A budget is a dashed line, drawn when within twice the data's top and otherwise said in words. A hollow point was taken under noise, and a dashed line is an estimate. The newest launch phases (`launch.phase.*`) are one bar split into phases, up to six with the smallest folded into "other". Every chart has a table under it.
4. **An empty store** shows the one command that fills it.

A trend reads a key's newest 30 runs and says how many there are. Times are UTC. What it costs each kept run, the median of 7 rewrites of 1,000 runs of 5 kinds each, at host load 44-47 on 15 cores (a measure takes seconds):

| runs by key           | rewrite | page     |
| --------------------- | ------- | -------- |
| 1,000 in 1 key        | 5 ms    | 84 KB    |
| 25 in each of 40 keys | 101 ms  | 2,365 KB |
| 5 in each of 200 keys | 207 ms  | 3,547 KB |

## Asking a kept trace

`perf_query` answers from a kept trace with no new recording: read-only SQL on a `.pftrace` through the pinned trace processor (optional
`INCLUDE PERFETTO MODULE` lines, then one `SELECT` or `WITH`), and on an Instruments `.trace` a table digested to its top rows (`table`; none
lists the tables). Rows are bounded, cells cut at 200 characters, and only files under the traces or measurements folders are read.

## The simulator's frame probe

On by default for `perf_scroll` and `perf_trace` on a simulator, and `probe: false` turns it off; `perf_launch` takes it on request, so that a kept launch stays one kind whatever else measured the app. `ios/SimHelper/SimProbe.m`, compiled on first use with clang (no binary ships), injected through `SIMCTL_CHILD_DYLD_INSERT_LIBRARIES`. The app relaunches once, unless it runs under the probe already, and the answer says so; the app is not rebuilt.

It runs a `CADisplayLink` on the main run loop and writes every frame interval, stalls over 100 ms, and when the app last finished handling a touch. Every result says what it is: main-thread pacing, not the render server or GPU, on the Mac's speed. It finds stalls and regressions on one machine; it does not predict a phone's frame rate, as [Apple says of simulators](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/running-your-app-on-simulated-or-physical-devices) that they do not replicate a device's performance.

A start through it (`perf_launch` with `trace`) writes its phases: `load`, process start to the library's first code; `main`, to UIKit's application object, so static initializers and what runs before `UIApplicationMain`, a SwiftUI app's `init` among them; `uikit`, to did finish launching; `first_frame`. A second start, held before its first instruction by `simctl launch --wait-for-debugger`, is profiled from there, and its busiest functions are in the answer.

## Thresholds the tools name

| issue                   | threshold                                                                                     | source                       |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| slow cold / warm launch | ≥ 5 s / ≥ 2 s                                                                                 | Android vitals (launch time) |
| frozen frame            | ≥ 700 ms                                                                                      | Android vitals (rendering)   |
| jank                    | median janky share of the scroll rounds (rounds under 60 frames left out) above 10 %          | this project's default       |
| memory growth           | over 30 % from the second round's end to the last's (the first round loads images and caches) | this project's default       |

An emulator's numbers depend on the host's load and GPU mode: they are relative and only compared with
the same kind of device (the tools say "sanal cihaz: göreli").

## Accuracy

`test-device/perf.test.ts` checks every measure against a fixture screen of known cost: a 2 s animation
whose draw takes about 1 ms or 40 ms (frames and the frame timeline), 50 MB of Java heap held (memory,
within 15 %), one busy core (CPU, 80–125 %), and cold launches against the system's own "Displayed" log
line (within 50 ms).

## iOS

A simulator app is a Mac process: its measures come from the Mac's own tools. A phone's come from Xcode's trace
tool (`xctrace`), which says when a recording has started (`--notify-tracing-started`, so a scroll runs inside it,
not after a guessed sleep) and is stopped at most 120 s past its limit. A start took 14-19 s and a stop 4-14 s on
a phone connected by USB, whatever the recording's length.
