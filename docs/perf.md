# Performance measurement

Every measurement is read from the platform's own tools and stored with the raw output it came from
(`<measurements dir>/pm-<id>.json` and `.txt`; the MCP server's `--measurements`, default
`~/Library/Caches/appdyno/measurements`). Commands run in the C locale. Nothing leaves the machine.

## What was tried, cell by cell

Xcode 27.1, iOS 27.0.1 on a cable, iOS 26.5 on the simulator, 2026-11-03. Host load beside what depends on it.

**Why launch was slow, simulator.** App Launch with `--launch` records (287 s at host load 30) and its `life-cycle-period` has 0 rows, so attaching was not what emptied it. What reads it is the probe, phase by phase, and a Time Profiler attached to a process that `simctl launch --wait-for-debugger` holds before its first instruction, let go with `SIGCONT` when the recording has started (16 s at load 14).

## The gate

Every measure's numbers are kept as records (`packages/perf`): kind, median, samples and spread, source command, raw output
file, device and OS, virtual or physical, build type, compile state, host and device load, thermal state, tool version and
time. A run goes to `<measurements dir>/runs/<app>/<scenario>/<device class>/`. A baseline goes to
`.appdyno/perf/baselines/<app>/<scenario>/<device class>.json` (`APPDYNO_PERF_DIR` moves it), which can be committed. Limits
go in `.appdyno/perf/budgets.json`: a list of `{ "kind": "launch.ttid_ms", "max": 2000 }`, with `app`, `scenario`, `deviceClass`
and `min` optional.

```text
appdyno perf-launch --app A --mode cold --repeats 6      a measured run, kept
appdyno perf baseline --app A                            adds the newest run to the baseline; at least 3 runs are needed
appdyno perf compare --app A [--format text|json|junit]  exit 1 on a regression or a broken budget
appdyno perf budget --app A                              budgets only
```

The same comes through `perf_compare`.

**How a change is called.** All of: at least 5 samples each side and 3 baseline runs, else `few-samples`, which is no alarm; a rank
test on all baseline samples against the run, p under 0.01 (by permutation, because whole milliseconds tie; 4 against 4 cannot reach
it); a shift past a floor by unit (15 % and 10 ms for times); and past 4 standard deviations of the baseline's run medians. A run under
high load or on a hot device says `noisy` and exit 0, and is refused as a baseline; a number of its own under noise is left out of one and named, and the rest of the run goes in. Unlike builds or compile states say `refused`.

**Why that method.** Samples of one run share its load and heat, so two runs of an unchanged app differ "significantly". Across 72
ordered pairs of unchanged runs, a rank test alone and a 10 % difference of medians alone would have alarmed:

| device, clean runs                           | pairs | rank test alone | 10 % of medians alone | the gate's calls, 6 runs |
| -------------------------------------------- | ----- | --------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Android emulator, 6 runs at load 9-16        | 30    | 10              | 24                    | 0                        |
| iOS simulator, 5 runs at load 9-13           | 20    | 2               | 0                     | 0                        |
| iPhone, 9 runs at any host load              | 72    | 10              | 9                     | 0                        |
| iOS simulator, an earlier 9 runs, load 10-15 | 72    | 28              | 25                    | 0                        |

Run medians of the unchanged app: emulator 371-667 ms, simulator 1,661-1,705 ms, iPhone 554-626 ms. On an emulator the same launch read
350 ms and 700 ms minutes apart between load 11 and 16 on 15 cores, and one baseline run at load 41 read 1,661 ms; so a run with host load over
0.8 per core is marked noisy and is left out of a baseline. So is a phone's number that is timed on the Mac (an iPhone's launch, to the first screen it reads), and not what a phone reports itself.

Why 4 standard deviations of run medians rather than their range, from `scripts/perf-floor-sim.mjs` (a run moving 6 % by itself, 5 % noise per
launch, 6 launches, 400 trials): false alarms 3.0 % with 3 baseline runs and 2.3 % with 5 for the range, 0.8 % and 0.5 % for 4 sd; +40 % caught
100 % and 99 % against 89 % and 91 %. At 10 % movement no floor is safe with 3 runs (4 sd still 5.8 %), so use more runs on a loaded host, or
a quieter one. These are simulated rates, and what the device runs below show is no false alarm in 6 runs each, not a rate.

**The acceptance tests** (`test-device/perf-compare.test.ts`; the numbers are kept in `.ci/perf-gate.txt`), 6 launches per run, a baseline
of 3 runs, 6 unchanged runs, then the fixture's start made slower by a known amount:

| device            | baseline median | unchanged runs               | false alarms | injected at start | called                      |
| ----------------- | --------------- | ---------------------------- | ------------ | ----------------- | --------------------------- |
| Android emulator  | 491 ms          | 366-764 ms, floor 509 ms     | 0 of 6       | 800 ms            | regression, +553 ms, exit 1 |
| iOS simulator     | 1,673 ms        | 1,659-1,787 ms, floor 251 ms | 0 of 6       | 1,000 ms          | regression, +983 ms, exit 1 |
| iPhone 13 Pro Max | 563 ms          | 553-620 ms, floor 84 ms      | 0 of 6       | 400 ms            | regression, +471 ms, exit 1 |

On 2026-11-03 the iPhone's gate passed once and, at host load 10-38 with other work on the Mac, called one unchanged run in another: 623 ms against 540.5, a shift of 83.5 over a floor of 81. A third run had 5 of 5 unchanged runs ok, then reached its hour's limit at host load over 100. So 0 of 6 is what those runs show, not a rate. The floor was left as it is.

On a Xiaomi the gate gave no verdict: its device load read 14-16 each time, over the limit of 8 (`DEVICE_LOAD` in `packages/perf/src/record.ts`), and its run answered `new`, with no baseline. At rest it read 14.31 with one task running and `procs_blocked` 0: the load was 14 kernel threads in uninterruptible sleep (`wdtk-0` to `wdtk-7`, `hang_detect`, `ccci_poll` and others), which Linux counts though they use no CPU, and `/proc/pressure/cpu` is closed to the shell there. A record now keeps that count, and the limit is applied to the load without it. Run again on 2026-11-04, at host load 250-370: a baseline of 900 ms, 6 unchanged runs of 837-928 ms against a floor of 135 ms and no false alarm, and an injected 800 ms called a regression, +723.5 ms, exit 1. An Android phone's launches do not wait for the Mac in this test, as what `am start -W` reports is the phone's. An iPhone's do: its time runs until the runner reads the first screen, and read 550 ms on a calm Mac and 1.3-3 s at host load 100-600. On 2026-11-04 its shortened gate at load 57-125 called one unchanged run in six a regression, 83 ms past a floor of 1,015 ms, and at load 50-370 missed an injected 400 ms under a floor of 370 ms: at that load it says nothing either way.

An injected change has to clear what that device's runs do by themselves, so a smaller one on a loaded simulator is not called: it is
reported as `ok`, with the floor beside it. On an emulator a sleep at the start of a very fast launch shows only partly: 400 ms added
about 230 ms, because the system's own start work runs alongside it.

## Kept traces

Traces and heap dumps stay on this machine, up to a limit: 10 GB by default, `--trace-limit-gb` or `APPDYNO_TRACE_LIMIT_GB` to change it. `appdyno perf prune [--limit-gb N] [--dry-run]` or `perf_compare` `action: prune` brings them down to it and lists what it removed; a dry run lists the same and touches nothing. Each removal goes in `traces-pruned.log` in the state folder, with its time and size. The server and the command line also look when a run is kept, at most once an hour. Nothing else that makes a context does: not the library, and not a test.

They go oldest first, and never what a baseline took its numbers from, what one of a scenario's 30 newest runs names, or what is under a day old, so it can stay over the limit, and says so. A baseline holds samples and no path, so the way there is the baseline's run time to the kept run, the run to its measurement, and the measurement's two files to the trace. Taking a baseline also pins what it took, in `traces-pinned.json`, as one project cannot see another's baselines.

**What it does not know, it does not delete.** It goes by one state folder: it reads that folder's `measurements`, and removes only direct entries of that folder's `traces` and `measurements` that are traces or heap dumps. With no kept run to go by, or a run, a measurement, a baseline or the pins it cannot read, it removes nothing and says why. A context with `--measurements` elsewhere is refused: the traces are not its runs' to speak for. A link is not followed, and a linked `traces` folder stops it.

An earlier version read a context's own runs and deleted from the state folder, so a context with no runs saw every trace as free, and unit tests ran it on this Mac's real folder. Only the limit stopped it. So a unit test now has a state folder of its own, and `packages/appdyno/test/no-real-state.setup.ts` watches every file call in every worker: one on `~/Library/Caches/appdyno` throws, and fails the run at its end even if the error is swallowed. Proven both ways: a clean run exits 0, and a swallowed read exits 1 naming the call.

## Why a slow frame was slow

On Android, `perf_trace` records what each frame's main thread did and answers for the frames that missed their deadline (`android_frame_stats`, `overrun` over 0): how long a frame it spent waiting for a monitor (`android_monitor_contention`), in binder calls (`android_binder_txns`), waiting for the collector (`GC: Wait For Completion`), and from `thread_state` at work, waiting for a CPU, blocked in the kernel or asleep with nothing named holding it. A wait is also a sleep, so it is taken out of the sleep. One is named only at 10 ms a frame or more and 40 % of the time or more; otherwise the frames are unexplained, which is said, not guessed at. The window is the frame's `doFrame`, so a stall before it began, or on the render thread, reads as unexplained.

Each frame given a known 100 ms on the fixture, by `test-device/frame-cause.test.ts`, on the Xiaomi, Android 16, host load 19-22 (the phone's own trace):

| known cost in each frame                      | named                                     | read                   |
| --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| a monitor another thread holds for 100 ms     | a monitor lock, the holder and its method | 91.6 ms a frame, 96 %  |
| a synchronous call to a process taking 100 ms | binder calls, to that process             | 102.3 ms a frame, 97 % |
| allocating for 100 ms                         | the garbage collector                     | 93.9 ms a frame, 84 %  |
| a loop of 100 ms                              | at work on the main thread                | 101 ms a frame, 99 %   |
| a sleep of 40 ms                              | asleep, with nothing named holding it     | 40.4 ms a frame, 94 %  |
| none                                          | nothing: no frame missed                  |                        |

A first version of the binder case spent 100 ms in a framework call, and the answer named work on the main thread: 89 ms was the client's own, 14 ms the binder call. So a framework call that is slow in the app's process is named as work, which is what it is.

## Why a hang was a hang, and which function moved

**iPhone, busy or blocked.** `perf_trace` `what: hangs` answers each hang as Apple triages it. A Time Profiler trace holds no `thread-state` table on a phone, but it samples only a thread that runs, so the hung thread's samples inside a hang are how much of it was on the CPU: at half or more it was busy, in what ran most, and below that blocked, in what it ran last. Nothing new is recorded. The function is the app's innermost, an address where the build has no symbols, and `dsym` on `perf_trace` or `perf_profile` names it. By `test-device/measures-iphone.test.ts`, on the iPhone, iOS 27.2: a 500 ms sleep read blocked, 513.6 ms, 2.3 % on the CPU; a 500 ms loop read busy, 505.2 ms, 100 %, in `busyMainThread()`. Of a blocked thread the last sample may be thin: there, a Release build's closure read as `main`.

`xctrace symbolicate --dsym` answered "No dSYMs were found or relevant" for that phone's trace and the dSYM of the very build in it, UUID for UUID, given the bundle, its folder or the DWARF file. So `dsym`, a `.dSYM` or a folder of them, names frames with `atos`, from the addresses and the load address the export has, and refuses a dSYM of no build in the trace. It names what is read, not the trace on disk: `perf_query` still sees addresses. On a simulator it has only run in a unit test.

**A function-level diff.** `perf_compare` `action: functions` with two ids of `perf_profile`, or `appdyno perf functions --from pm-a --to pm-b`: what moved, in ms of CPU in each second so that windows of other lengths compare, the app's own functions where the profile has them. A function must move 20 ms a second and a quarter of itself, so two runs of the same work name nothing. A profile keeps 200 functions, and an answer shows 15. Android's names are what simpleperf finds on the device: Java functions of a debuggable build, once compiled, and the first run of a screen may read as the interpreter. By `test-device/function-diff.test.ts`, on the Xiaomi, host load 13: two runs of the same work named nothing, and 300 ms a second put into one function read `+296 knownCost`, first of the diff.

## Why an app started

On Android 15 and later, `perf_snapshot` with `also: startInfo` reads `dumpsys activity start-info`, with no trace: the reason of the newest start, its type, whether it followed a force stop, and where its time went, for the starts the system keeps (16 here). Each phase also goes to the row "Why launch was slow". Before Android 15 it is listed as not measurable here, with the reason kept. Index 2 of the dump is when `Application.onCreate` began, so the time inside `onCreate` is in `oncreate_to_first_frame`, not before it: a known 500 ms there took that phase from 386.9 to 874.8 ms on the Xiaomi, +488 ms, host load 5-108, by `test-device/start-info.test.ts`. On an emulator with API 36, at host load 170-340, the same sleep showed in part, as a sleep does there. A launcher start read `LAUNCHER`, and a service in a process of its own `SERVICE`. A broadcast to a stopped app read `BROADCAST` on the emulator; HyperOS completes that broadcast and starts nothing, so a Xiaomi has no such record. Reading it needs the app running, as `perf_snapshot` does: a process up for a broadcast counts, and reads 0 frames.

## A journey

`appdyno test <flow> --measure` or `flow` `run` with `iterations`. A first run is left out, as a measured launch leaves out its warm-up, and the iterations (5 by default: the gate calls nothing under 5 samples a side) make one kept run of the scenario `journey-<file>`:

| kind                                                   | what                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `journey.total_ms`                                     | each iteration's steps added up                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `journey.<n>_<line>.ms`                                | the device's own count from the action to a settled screen; `launch`, `open_link`, `scroll_to`, `wait_for`, `assert` and what has no such count are the action on this Mac's clock, `throughHost` |
| `journey.<n>_<line>.frames_p95_ms`, `.frames_worst_ms` | the step's frames: reset before its action, read after it settled; none when it drew nothing                                                                                                      |
| `journey.<n>_<line>.memory_kb`                         | what `perf_snapshot` reads, after it settled                                                                                                                                                      |

The reads are between the steps, so they cost time but are in no step's number. A step after which the app does not run has no reading, and the run goes on.

Against a known cost, by `test-device/journey.test.ts`, on the Xiaomi, Android 16, 2026-11-04, host load 27-33 on 15 cores: a baseline of 3 runs of 5 iterations, an unchanged run all `ok`, then 700 ms added to one step (`files/delayed_ms`): `regression journey.04_tap_id_load.ms: 1663 ms against 952, +709 ms, p 0.0002`, exit 1, and no other step called. What went through the Mac (`stop`, `launch`, `assert`, the whole) was left out of the baseline at that load and read `new`, and the step the phone timed itself was called all the same. 50 MB held in one step read as +50.1 MB in that step's memory; a step with 40 ms draws read frames p95 81 ms, worst 91 ms, against 10 and 11 for a light one. On the iPhone, iOS 27.2: an unchanged run read `ok`, 1,776 ms against 1,819, and 700 ms added to the step read `regression journey.03_tap_id_open_delayed.ms: 2498 ms against 1819, +713 ms, p 0.0002`, exit 1, on that step alone.

**A measure inside a flow.** A `perf_*` call during a recording used to be written as `unrecorded`, which stopped the replay. A measure is the agent measuring, not the journey, and a journey now has its numbers from `--measure`, so what it did to the screen stays as the plain steps that do the same and nothing stops: `perf_launch` cold is `stop`, `launch`, warm or hot is `launch`; `perf_scroll` is its swipes; `perf_trace` or `perf_profile` with `scroll` is 4 swipes; a relaunch for the probe, or XCTest's own test, is `stop`, `launch`. What leaves the screen as it found it writes nothing.

## No silent success

**A failed action says whether it touched anything.** Its answer ends "no touch was sent", "the touch was sent", or, for a device that stopped answering, "the touch may have been sent: read the screen before trying again", and a client has `dispatched`: `true`, `false` or `unknown`. A target that is not there and a refusal by the device come before any touch; a scroll that ran out of list swiped first.

**A touch looks first.** Every action reads the screen unless a read was taken since the last action and under 20 s ago, as a phone locks itself about a minute after the last touch: during research a blind swipe opened Spotlight from a lock screen. It costs a read only where there was none. A read that is refused leaves nothing to go on, so the next touch reads again. Within those 20 s a screen that locked while on is still touched; one that went dark is refused by the agent.

**A locked phone refuses by name**, with `DEVICE_LOCKED`, reads and touches alike. Android: after a wake and a dismissal, a keyguard that stays. On an emulator with API 36 and a PIN set for the test, at host load 220-250, `screen`, `tap` and `swipe` refused and no touch went out, by `test-device/guards.test.ts`. An iPhone with no passcode gets past its lock screen with the home button: on a dark screen the first press only wakes it, with the lock screen still up, so it presses up to three times, and the app comes back. A swipe 90 s after the last touch reached the app after one press, a launch waking the screen, and a session with no app chosen got past it on its own, on the iPhone. With a passcode the read refuses, by `devicectl device info lockState`, which reads `passcodeRequired: false` on this phone; so that refusal is from the code alone.

**Two recordings at once refuse by name.** Xcode's trace tool, given a second recording, fails to lock the performance counters and leaves an empty trace, so `recordTrace` holds a device across processes, and a second gets `DEVICE_CLAIMED` naming the first, a dead holder's file being taken over. On the iPhone the second refused and the first kept its trace. A recording by another tool shows only as that failure, which refuses the same way. Android's Perfetto takes sessions side by side, so it has no such rule.

**An iPhone's thermal state.** No tool answers this by itself, but every recording but a lone Power Profiler instrument holds `device-thermal-state-intervals`, so a measure reads the state its recording ended in, up to 5 minutes old, on Android's scale that a run's noise is judged by: nominal 0, fair 1, serious 2, critical 4. A measure with no recording in the last 5 minutes, such as a launch alone, has none. On the iPhone it read 0.

## The dashboard

```text
appdyno perf survey --app A [--device S]               every measure on that device in turn, then the page
appdyno perf dashboard [--app A] [--out dir] [--open]  the page alone, from what is kept; no device needed
```

`perf_compare` with `action: dashboard` writes the page too, in its usual place: `<measurements dir>/dashboard/index.html`. `--out` and `--open` are the command line's alone. The page is rewritten after each kept run and each baseline, and an open page reloads itself every minute, which closes a table left open. A failure to write it never fails a measurement.

`perf survey` calls the same operations anyone can, and names no device: what reads each measure is tried in turn until the measure has a number, a refusal is kept as that measure's answer for that app on that kind of device, and the next measure still runs. Every operation keeps its refusals the same way, except what the caller turned off (`probe: false`) and a way the device has another of (no `hangs` where the frame trace reads stalls); a refusal about a part of a row (threads, a CPU profile) stands for no row. A call that ran and kept no number for that measure, or failed for another reason such as a timeout, is printed and not kept: neither says what the device cannot do. One line per measure.

There is no server, and the page is one file with no script, which loads nothing: its content policy allows only its own style. Everything from the store is escaped. It follows the system's light or dark, and prints as ink on paper with no tooltips or tables. An app has a section of its own, with an anchor. In order:

1. **What needs attention**, across every app, for the newest run of each scenario: regressions and broken budgets as `perf compare` calls them, and runs under high load or heat. A debuggable build is said beside its number but is no alarm. It says how many scenarios have a baseline and how many budget rules there are, since "nothing needs attention" means little without them, and which files could not be read.
2. **Measures by device.** The row `Journeys` holds the newest journey on that device, its whole time, its slowest step and the steps the gate calls slower. Above the table: that an emulator's or a simulator's numbers are relative to this Mac and that real frame rates, frozen frames, hitches and energy need a phone, and, with no phone measured for the app, the command for one. A cell holds the newest reading of that measure on that kind of device, with its unit, kind, scenario and age; an estimate from a model is marked; so is a reading under noise; and on a simulator it says when the number is something else: main-thread pacing for frames, frozen frames and stalls, this Mac's energy. A refusal newer than any reading gives its reason instead. Otherwise "not measured yet". A kind no measure answers has a row of its own under "other". Emulators and simulators read as relative, and the page says so.
3. **Trends**, for a kind with more than one run on a device: one small chart per kind, scenario and device, each with one y-scale, from zero, with the runs evenly spaced. The shaded band is the baseline's median plus and minus the floor of the gate above: a run outside it has moved past the floor, though the call needs the rank test as well. A budget is a dashed line, drawn when within twice the data's top and otherwise said in words. A hollow point was taken under noise, and a dashed line is an estimate. The newest launch phases (`launch.phase.*`) are one bar split into phases, up to six with the smallest folded into "other", and each journey's newest run one bar split by step, with a table of each step's time, verdict, frames and memory; a journey's whole has a trend, and so has a step the gate calls slower. Every chart has a table under it.
4. **An empty store** shows the one command that fills it.

A trend reads a key's newest 30 runs and says how many there are. Times are UTC. What it costs each kept run, the median of 7 rewrites of 1,000 runs of 5 kinds each, at host load 44-47 on 15 cores (a measure takes seconds):

| runs by key           | rewrite | page     |
| --------------------- | ------- | -------- |
| 1,000 in 1 key        | 5 ms    | 84 KB    |
| 25 in each of 40 keys | 101 ms  | 2,365 KB |
| 5 in each of 200 keys | 207 ms  | 3,547 KB |

## Asking a kept trace

`perf_query` answers from a kept trace with no new recording: read-only SQL on a `.pftrace` through the pinned trace processor (optional
`INCLUDE PERFETTO MODULE` lines, then one `SELECT` or `WITH`), and on an Instruments `.trace` a table digested to its top rows (`table`; none
lists the tables). Rows are bounded, cells cut at 200 characters, and only files under the traces or measurements folders are read.

## The simulator's frame probe

On by default for `perf_scroll` and `perf_trace` on a simulator, and `probe: false` turns it off; `perf_launch` takes it on request, so that a kept launch stays one kind whatever else measured the app. `ios/SimHelper/SimProbe.m`, compiled on first use with clang (no binary ships), injected through `SIMCTL_CHILD_DYLD_INSERT_LIBRARIES`. The app relaunches once, unless it runs under the probe already, and the answer says so; the app is not rebuilt.

It runs a `CADisplayLink` on the main run loop and writes every frame interval, stalls over 100 ms, and when the app last finished handling a touch. Every result says what it is: main-thread pacing, not the render server or GPU, on the Mac's speed. It finds stalls and regressions on one machine; it does not predict a phone's frame rate, as [Apple says of simulators](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/running-your-app-on-simulated-or-physical-devices) that they do not replicate a device's performance.

A start through it (`perf_launch` with `trace`) writes its phases: `load`, process start to the library's first code; `main`, to UIKit's application object, so static initializers and what runs before `UIApplicationMain`, a SwiftUI app's `init` among them; `uikit`, to did finish launching; `first_frame`. A second start, held before its first instruction by `simctl launch --wait-for-debugger`, is profiled from there, and its busiest functions are in the answer.

## Accuracy

`test-device/perf.test.ts` checks every measure against a fixture screen of known cost: a 2 s animation
whose draw takes about 1 ms or 40 ms (frames and the frame timeline), 50 MB of Java heap held (memory,
